A number went up by one. There is no address attached to it, no account, nothing about you and nothing about this visit. It says how many, never who, and it is the only number this site keeps.

Most sites would have asked for your email here. We did not, because this site tells you it keeps nothing, and collecting addresses on the way to that promise would make it untrue. Counting how many people were interested answers the question well enough, and it means there is nothing to leak, nothing to sell and nothing to unsubscribe from.

The privacy page says the same thing, and this is the number it is talking about.

What the app would be.

The same tools, without a browser around them. That would mean no size limit worth mentioning, since a desktop application is not held to what a browser tab can keep in memory, and files that open straight from your computer rather than being chosen through a picker.

What would not change is the part that matters. It would still do the work on your own machine and still send nothing anywhere, because that is the point of the thing rather than a feature of it.

Whether it happens.

Honestly, it depends on this number. Building and maintaining a desktop application for three operating systems is a great deal more work than a website, and it is worth doing only if people actually want it. If the count stays small, the website stays as it is, which is no bad outcome: it already does the job.

In the meantime everything here works today, on this device, with no account and no limit. The tools are on the home page.
